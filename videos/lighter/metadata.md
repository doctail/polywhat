## Description
What if your mass halved every single hour, and nothing could stop it?

Hour 2, one small jump puts your head into the ceiling. Hour 5, a gust of wind carries you down the street. Hour 10, you're lighter than air and you start to rise. Hour 31, sunlight itself pushes you out of the atmosphere. Hour 59, you pass Voyager 1. And less than six days after you stepped on a scale, you rise out of the Milky Way while nineteen thousand years pass on Earth.

All of it from one rule: 70 kg ÷ 2, once an hour.

The numbers: mass = 70 kg ÷ 2^hour, while your body stays the same size. Altitude is where the air is as dense as you are. In space, sunlight pushes you (about 4.6 µN per m² near Earth, weaker with distance and with your speed), and the flight is worked out with special relativity: "hours" are your hours, and Earth time comes from time dilation. Comparisons are approximate.
